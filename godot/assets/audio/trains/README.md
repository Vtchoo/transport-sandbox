# Train audio

`sounds.json` maps catalog IDs to optional engine, steam-chuff and rolling loops. The shared `rolling` array supplies a random wheel sound per vehicle; model-specific `rolling` overrides it. The chosen sample stays stable when a vehicle leaves and re-enters audio range during a session. Random starting offsets and ±2.5% pitch variation reduce synchronized phasing. Paths are relative to this folder. Use mono WAV files for positional playback; M4A needs conversion first. Missing files produce a warning and disable that layer.

The mixer defaults to 100% volume, with conservative per-layer gains. Settings → Sound provides master, engine, wheel and curve-whine volume sliders and a mute toggle. Changes apply immediately and persist in `user://audio-settings.cfg`, shared by gameplay and the menu. Engine pitch and volume follow effort/speed, wheels fade in with speed, and flange whine follows speed and bogie-tangent curvature. Steam chuffs play A–D in sequence at four beats per driver revolution, using the same wheel turns as the animated rods. Effort controls volume, while Doppler affects pitch independently of cadence. One-shot players are pooled per audible locomotive, support overlapping tails and stop on mute/removal; old beats are discarded after relocation or a hitch. Audio uses the main camera only, including Doppler from both vehicle and camera velocity in metres per second. Floating-origin rebases reset motion history; teleport velocities are discarded. Preview and window cameras do not duplicate sounds. The nearest 16 vehicles within 350 m receive voices; pause and scene teardown follow Godot's node lifecycle.

## Engine pitch and volume

Diesel/electric engines use OpenLoco's sound controller from `VehicleHead.cpp` (MIT; see `godot/third-party/OpenLoco-LICENSE.txt`). `engine-profiles.json` stores imported parameters in Hz and metres/second. The `engineProfile` key in `sounds.json` assigns profiles to catalog vehicles. `scripts/export-engine-audio.py` regenerates the profiles and independent fixed-point reference fixtures from the extracted vehicle metadata.

Simple motors choose idle, powered and coasting frequency/volume targets. Powered frequency adds a speed-dependent increment; braking uses the idle target. Frequency and volume approach targets at the original per-tick rates, with the original quiet, low-pitch startup. Friction motors are silent below their minimum speed, then increase frequency/volume directly with speed. Gearbox support applies configured speed thresholds and frequency drops, including reduced volume while pitch falls. The clock uses fixed 31 ms updates independent of rendering, and OpenLoco's 22,000 Hz pitch reference and local vehicle-volume conversion. Godot supplies spatial attenuation; our camera-relative Doppler multiplies the result afterward. No additional engine interpolation is applied on top of the original ramps. Long frame stalls have bounded catch-up.

F7 uses the CLW Santa Fe F7A (`USF7A25`) parameters. The GE engines use `USATP383`, a CLW GE Dash 8 using the same SNDDSH9E recording, as a test stand-in. TGV uses the Acela (`USATACEL`) friction profile accompanying SNDACLEN. Steam keeps fixed-pitch idle and separate driver-synchronized chuffs. Movement publishes stopped/powered/coasting/braking states from the real target speed, including station/signal braking; audio does not change simulation, physics or routing. Wheel slip is not currently simulated. `EngineAudioChecks` compares 1,340 updates against the original fixed-point reference and checks 60/144 FPS agreement.

## Station horns

Station visits sound a horn on approach (within roughly five seconds or 60 m of the stop), once per visit. Departures have a 50% horn chance, adjustable with `departureHornChance` in `sounds.json`; the roll happens once after the stopped train starts moving. Both ordered and wandering trains use these events. Signals alone do not trigger departure horns. Horns/whistles are optional per-model `horn` paths, play as one-shots with spatial attenuation and Doppler, follow their locomotive and respect master/engine volume and mute. Eight simultaneous horn voices are allowed, without overlapping the same vehicle's horn.

Temporary horn mappings: F7 uses SNDFP7HO, GE locomotives use SND1CHIM, TGV uses SNDACLHO, and Rio Grande uses the SND412W1 steam whistle. These are test stand-ins, not exact prototype recordings.

## Temporary assets

`testing/` contains replaceable development assets. `sources.json` identifies the source objects and packs. F7 uses FP7 audio, Dash 9 uses its named sample, GE 809 shares that GE sample, and TGV uses an Acela electric stand-in. All vehicles' wheels randomly use SNDFRGH2 or SNDFRGHT. Rio Grande uses SNDS1 for idle steam (also in the menu) and SNDCH1A–D for individual chuffs. Its old SNDTR3 puffing wheel loop and procedural chuffs are no longer assigned. The covered-station/tunnel AS–DS variants are extracted in the listening gallery but are not assigned: the game does not yet model those acoustic environments. These are test mappings, not claims of exact recordings for each prototype.

Flange whine is a procedural placeholder generated by `scripts/prepare-train-audio.py`. The script also converts the selected recordings to mono, crossfades loop boundaries, fades one-shot edges and normalizes peaks to -6 dBFS. Regeneration requires both `.files/locomotion/extracted-audio/manifest.json` (CLW) and `.files/locomotion/objdata-audio/manifest.json` (base ObjData), with their WAV files. Per-model `chuffs` arrays configure the sequential one-shots separately from wheel noise. Replace these temporary assets with licensed recordings before release.
